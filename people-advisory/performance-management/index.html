Service scope

Performance Management

A performance management process only creates value when it changes something, a development conversation, a promotion decision, a role change, rather than an annual review cycle everyone completes because HR requires it and nobody actually acts on afterward.

A business based in Al Muruj running the same annual review cycle as everyone else usually finds the same thing: reviews get completed because HR requires them, then nobody acts on what they said.

This is the ongoing discipline that measures performance against the roles established through organization and role design, and it works best when built around the specific capabilities an HR strategy has already identified as genuinely strategic for the business.

Why annual-only reviews genuinely fail to drive performance

A single formal conversation once a year, disconnected from the actual work happening the other fifty-one weeks, tends to become a compliance exercise focused on completing a form correctly rather than a genuine tool for improving how someone actually performs in their role.

What a system that actually drives performance looks like

Clear expectations set at the start of a period rather than assessed only at the end, regular informal check-ins that catch problems while they're still fixable, and a formal review that summarizes an ongoing conversation rather than delivering feedback for the first time in that single meeting.

A common Saudi scenario

A Riyadh-headquartered company applying the same generic review template across a fast-growing services team and a stable, technically skilled Riyadh manufacturing team often finds the same process doesn't actually work well for both, since the two teams are genuinely driven by different performance factors.

What we deliver

A performance management process matched to your actual organizational reality, not a generic corporate template borrowed from a much larger company, with the specific manager training needed to make ongoing conversations happen consistently rather than only once a year. This connects back to organization and role design whenever the review process itself surfaces roles that need reconsidering.

Making the shift from annual event to ongoing habit

The hardest part of this change is usually cultural, not procedural. Managers who've only ever done one formal review a year need genuine support and modeling from leadership to build the habit of frequent, lower-stakes conversations instead.

Local context

Fast-growing Riyadh services teams typically need a performance process emphasizing frequent, informal feedback given how quickly roles and expectations shift.

Common questions

Performance Management, answered

How often should performance conversations happen?

Informal check-ins monthly or even more frequently, with a formal, documented review at least twice a year, since a purely annual cycle leaves too much time for issues to compound before anyone addresses them directly.

How do we get managers to actually have these conversations?

Through specific training and, critically, by holding managers themselves accountable for the quality and frequency of these conversations as part of their own performance expectations, not just providing a template and hoping it gets used.

Does this need to be tied to compensation decisions?

It should inform them, but the developmental conversation and the compensation conversation are best kept somewhat distinct, since conflating the two too tightly tends to make people defensive during development discussions rather than genuinely open to feedback.
